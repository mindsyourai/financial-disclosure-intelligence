The big idea — three takeaways

1 · Different models see different evidence

Do not collapse that evidence too early. LM sees words; FinBERT sees sentences; context sees history and peers.

2 · Disagreement can be diagnostically useful

Ask why methods diverge — and whether the conflict is resolvable. A disagreement with a diagnosis is evidence; without one, it is just noise.

3 · Decision support is more than accuracy

Traceability, uncertainty, context, and human attention are part of the output. A system that hides its uncertainty is selling false precision.

  • Brown & Tucker (2011), “Large-Sample Evidence on Firms' Year-over-Year MD&A Modifications”, Journal of Accounting Research 49(2), 309–348 — measures how much MD&A changes year over year; MD&A usefulness declines as boilerplate grows.

How to read the results

Every ADAM result on this site is labelled PRELIMINARY — VALIDATION ONGOING. The evaluation uses a 300-paragraph human reference set; confirmatory testing, confidence intervals, and paired significance tests are still to come in the final paper. Treat the numbers as the current validation picture, not as final claims.

Probabilities in the FinBERT worked example (0.62 / 0.30 / 0.08) are illustrative teaching values, not a real model run. 10-K passages marked “illustrative paraphrase” are written in the style of a filing, not quoted from any company.

A note on the numbers

The study's focal set is 143,372 paragraphs from Item 1A and Item 7. List sizes for the LM dictionary are approximate and version-dependent.

The (Positive − Negative) ÷ (Positive + Negative) = −0.091 figure is Henry's (2008) tone measure, shown for comparison — it is not LM 2011's formula.

Where it gets tricky — and why that's normal

Words that look negative

Tax, cost, capital, board, vice: a general dictionary flags them; finance does not. Always ask which dictionary produced a score.

Two formulas, one sign

LM's net tone (−0.02) and Henry's measure (−0.091) agree on direction and disagree on magnitude. The formula matters as much as the words.

A clear label, a weak confidence

FinBERT can say “Negative” at 0.62 confidence. The label is not the confidence — and below 0.80, ADAM sends it to a human.

Judge-only is already strong

On sentiment and direction the LLM alone does most of the work. The architecture's proven gain is informativeness — say so honestly.

Humans disagree too

Three careful reviewers agree unanimously only 21% of the time on sentiment. That is a fact about the task, not a failure of the study.
